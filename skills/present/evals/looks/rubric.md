# Looks rubric

Score how the rendered page looks and reads, not whether its facts are right. Each criterion is scored 1 to 5; the anchors fix 1, 3 and 5, and 2 and 4 sit between them. Criteria 1 to 6 apply to every page; for a deck case, read "page" as "the deck" and score them over the slides. Criteria 7 and 8 apply to decks only. Name the screenshot each score rests on.

Some criteria carry a check: a yes or no question answered before the score, with the screenshot it rests on and one line on what you saw. Answer the checks first; they are read before the scores. Judge charts and diagrams from the figure screenshots (`-fig-`), not from the overviews, which may catch a chart mid-animation.

## 1. first_screen_impact: first-screen impact

What the reader gets from the first 1440x900 screen, before scrolling (the title slide and slide 2 for a deck).

- **1**: The first screen is chrome, a title and a block of text or a table of contents. Nothing says what the page concludes or why it matters.
- **3**: The takeaway is on the first screen, but as one sentence among several of the same weight, or the one visual there is decoration rather than the point.
- **5**: Within five seconds the reader knows the conclusion: a clear headline or summary, and a figure, stat or picture that carries the main point, with room around it.

Check `point_in_three_seconds`: After a three-second look at the first screen, can you state the page's point? Put the point as you read it in the note.

Check `figure_on_first_screen`: Is there a figure (a chart, diagram, graph, stat or image) on the first screen?

## 2. hierarchy: hierarchy

Whether size, weight and position show what matters most, then next, then detail.

- **1**: Everything has the same weight: headings barely differ from body, important and minor content look alike, the eye has no entry point.
- **3**: Headings and sections are clear, but within sections the key point competes with detail (a key number buried in a paragraph or a table).
- **5**: There is one obvious entry point per screen and a clear order after it; the key claim of each section is the most prominent thing in it, detail is visibly secondary.

## 3. rhythm_density: rhythm and density

How text, figures and white space alternate down the page, judged from the overview and the screens.

- **1**: Long walls of text or stacked tables with no visual break; or the reverse, so sparse that a screen carries almost nothing.
- **3**: Some alternation, but at least one stretch of more than a screen of uninterrupted text, or a cluster of boxes crammed together.
- **5**: A steady rhythm: every screen has a break (a figure, a stat row, a short list, a heading) and none is crowded; white space groups related things.

## 4. figure_quality_fit: figure quality and fit

Charts, diagrams, the graph, stats and images: whether they are legible, finished and the right kind for the point.

- **1**: Figures are missing where the content is numeric or structural, or they are broken, empty, half-drawn, unreadable (tiny labels, overlapping text) or the wrong kind for the data.
- **3**: Figures render and are readable, but at least one is decorative, too dense, mislabelled, or a table or list would have served better (or a figure would have served better than a table).
- **5**: Each figure answers one question the text raises, is the right kind for it, reads at a glance (labels, units, legend only where needed) and sits next to the sentence that interprets it.

Check `charts_labelled_directly`: Are chart lines and bars labelled directly, with names or values on or beside the marks rather than only in a legend? Answer n/a when the page has no chart.

## 5. restraint: restraint in colour and emphasis

Colour, bold, callouts, panels and tones used to point, not to decorate.

- **1**: Colour and emphasis everywhere: many tones or accents with no meaning, much bold, callouts and boxes on most blocks, so nothing stands out.
- **3**: Mostly calm, with one or two areas of over-emphasis (a run of coloured panels, several callouts in a row, bold in every line).
- **5**: Colour encodes meaning consistently (one accent, semantic colours only where they mean something), emphasis is rare and lands on the one thing that matters; both themes stay readable.

Check `colour_for_one_meaning`: Is colour used for one thing that matters, and not for decoration?

## 6. dyslexic_scannability: scannability for a dyslexic reader

Can a dyslexic reader skim it and find what they need: short lines and paragraphs, plain words, numbers where the eye finds them.

- **1**: Long dense paragraphs, long lines, numbers inside sentences, jargon or long words, small or low-contrast text, no way to skim.
- **3**: Mostly short blocks, but some long paragraphs or numbers buried in prose; headings help but do not tell the story alone.
- **5**: Headings alone tell the story; paragraphs are one to three short sentences; numbers sit in stats, tables or charts; lists are short; contrast and size are comfortable in both themes.

## 7. one_idea_per_slide: one idea per slide (decks only)

- **1**: Most slides carry several points, a full paragraph, or a list of more than five items; slides read like a document.
- **3**: Most slides carry one idea, but several are crowded or split their attention between two blocks of equal weight.
- **5**: Every slide makes one claim in its heading and shows one thing that supports it; nothing on a slide competes with that.

Check `slide_titles_are_claims` (decks only): Is each slide title a full-sentence claim? Name the first title that is not in the note.

Check `one_idea_each_slide` (decks only): Does each slide carry one idea? Name the first slide that carries more in the note.

## 8. room_legibility: room legibility (decks only)

Whether the slides read from the back of a meeting room on a projector.

- **1**: Body text, labels or chart text are small enough that they would not read from a few metres; low contrast; content runs to the slide edges.
- **3**: Headings read from the back, but some body text, chart labels or diagram boxes are too small or too many.
- **5**: Every word on every slide reads from the back: large type, few words, figures with big labels, strong contrast in both themes.
